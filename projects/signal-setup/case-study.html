Set up

Signal Setup

A phone flow that lets school staff set up a speed-test box on their own.

Open the prototype

Not yet tested with users The people and the journey below are assumptions; the test plan at the end is how I would check them.

Problem

In Classroom Signal, 15 of the 128 schools have no speed test, so nobody knows whether they are online. A school only gets a test when someone there sets up a test box: usually a head teacher, a teacher or a caretaker who may read little English or French and uses a cheap Android phone with prepaid data.

Goal: set up the box alone, on your own phone, in about 10 minutes, typing nothing longer than a short code.

People Hypothetical

N

Neema, head teacher

Primary school, Tobani district

Context
No IT staff, the router is in her office, power cuts most weeks. Entry-level Android, data bought in small bundles.
Needs
To set the box up once, correctly, without spending her own data.
So the design must
Never ask for what she does not know, and survive interruptions.
S

Samuel, ICT teacher

Secondary school, Dasaro district

Context
Looks after the computer lab; comfortable with apps.
Needs
The real numbers, and real steps when something fails.
So the design must
Offer details on request without slowing others down.
J

Joseph, caretaker

Primary school, Ruvazi district

Context
Often the one at school when deliveries arrive. Reads short words and numbers; prefers listening.
Needs
To do what he was asked without making a mistake.
So the design must
Show a picture for every step, read aloud, and need no typing beyond a code.

Journey

Neema, from the day the box arrives to the weeks after setup.

  1. The box arrives

    Doing
    Signs for a parcel from the district office.
    Thinking
    “What is this for? Will it cost the school money?”
    Feeling
    Unsure
    Design response
    A one-page picture guide in the box says what it is, that it is free, and shows the code to scan.

  2. Getting ready

    Doing
    Looks for the router and a free power socket.
    Thinking
    “The only socket in the office is used by the fridge.”
    Feeling
    Worried
    Design response
    The first screen lists what is needed. Progress is saved, so she can stop and come back.

  3. Opening the setup

    Doing
    Scans the code on the box with her phone camera.
    Thinking
    “Will this use up my data?”
    Feeling
    Cautious
    Design response
    A small web page, no app. Typing the code is offered if the camera fails.

  4. Checking the school

    Doing
    Sees her school’s name and district.
    Thinking
    “Yes, that is us.”
    Feeling
    Reassured
    Design response
    The box is assigned before it is sent, so she recognises the school instead of typing an ID.

  5. Connecting

    Doing
    Plugs in the network cable, then the power.
    Thinking
    “Which hole does this go in?”
    Feeling
    Unsure, then relieved
    Design response
    One picture per step, everyday words, and a light that tells her it is starting.

  6. Waiting

    Doing
    Waits about 2 minutes for the box to start.
    Thinking
    “Is it working?”
    Feeling
    Impatient
    Design response
    The wait is used to ask for a phone number, with a live status line.

  7. First result

    Doing
    Sees the first speed test.
    Thinking
    “Is our school bad?”
    Feeling
    Anxious, then calmer
    Design response
    Shows what the school can do, not only Mbps, and says the result is about the connection.

  8. Weeks later

    Doing
    Someone unplugs the box to charge a phone.
    Thinking
    “Nobody told me it must stay on.”
    Feeling
    Unaware
    Design response
    The last screen and the box label say “keep it plugged in”, and an SMS goes out after 2 days offline.

Sketches, and what changed

The changes came from my own review against usability heuristics, not from users.

Screens

Details

  • No app to install: one small page, opened from the code on the box.
  • The box is assigned to a school before it is sent, so people confirm a name instead of typing an ID.
  • “Listen” reads each screen aloud with the phone’s own voice.
  • The Android back button goes to the previous step, and progress is saved on the phone.
  • “Our school has no internet” is a full path, so an unknown school becomes a known one.
  • Every control is at least 48 px tall. Checked with axe-core on all 17 screens in both languages and with Lighthouse; not yet tested with screen-reader users.

Test plan Planned, not yet run

Six school staff at their own schools, with their own phones and a real box, and a moderator who speaks their language. About 40 minutes each, with consent read aloud.

TaskSuccess looks like
“This box arrived from the district. Please set it up.”Done without help in about 12 minutes, cables in the right sockets.
A box addressed to another school is handed over.Notices the wrong school and stops.
The box is unplugged from the router beforehand.Finds and fixes the cable with the light check.
“Can your school stream a video lesson?”Answers correctly from the result screen.
Interrupted half-way for 5 minutes.Picks up from the saved progress.

Ready to build when at least 5 of 6 people finish without help, nobody sets up a box sent to another school, and the median time is 12 minutes or less.
