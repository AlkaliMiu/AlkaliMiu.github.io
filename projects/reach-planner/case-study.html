Problem

86 of the 128 schools in Classroom Signal are offline or below the target. With limited money, a ministry has to decide which to fix first and how. The answer depends on distance to the fibre network, mobile coverage, cost over several years, and fairness. Planning officers, regulators and providers each need to see it differently.

Two ways to choose

Most students per dollar

35 schools, 27,409 students

13 of the 30 offline schools.

Furthest behind first

27 schools, 19,350 students

26 of the 30 offline schools.

Both with $400,000 over 5 years and a 20 Mbps target. The planner always states what the other rule would do, and its chart shows both at every budget; they meet at about $1.25 million, when all 86 schools are connected.

How a plan is made

  1. Take the schools that are offline or below the target. Schools with no speed test are left out and pointed to Signal Setup.
  2. Measure each school’s distance to the fibre backbone and check 4G coverage on the map.
  3. Price every option over 5 years and pick the cheapest one that meets the target.
  4. Order the schools by the chosen rule.
  5. Go down the list, skipping any school that no longer fits the money left.

The last step is not the optimal selection, but an officer can explain it in one sentence, and each school says how much more money it would need.

Details

  • Budget, priority rule and target sit in one row above everything they change.
  • The map can show the fibre backbone, 4G coverage and planned links as separate layers.
  • Work packages group the plan by district and technology, and copy as CSV.
  • Every cost can be edited, for example to count 10 years instead of 5.
  • English and French; the school list is the keyboard and screen-reader path. Checked with axe-core in 30 states and with Lighthouse; not yet tested with screen-reader users.

Validation Planned, not yet run

  • Three or four planners or regulators: find what $500,000 reaches, explain why one school is left out, and prepare one district’s packages.
  • A network engineer reviews the cost model and whether fibre must follow roads.
  • A comparison with an optimal selection, to measure what the simple rule gives up.
